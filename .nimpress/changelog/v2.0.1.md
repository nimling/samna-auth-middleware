## Byte ranges on asset data

The asset data route answered whole files only, so audio and video could not seek and a client that asked for a range got the full body every time. Every hop now answers ranges the way a file server does.

- An answer whose size is known carries `Accept-Ranges: bytes`.
- One range or a suffix range answers `206` with `Content-Range` and only those bytes.
- `If-Range` with the current strong `ETag` or the exact `Last-Modified` keeps the range; any other validator answers the whole body with `200`.
- A range that starts past the last byte answers `416` with `Content-Range: bytes */<size>`.
- Several ranges in one request answer the whole body with `200`.

## Every hop

- The home cuts the range from its `AssetSource` body, seeking a body that is an `io.Seeker` and reading up to the range otherwise.
- The prophet reads only the range from its blob store for an asset it holds, and passes `Range` and `If-Range` to the home for one it relays, answering the home's `206` or `416` as it came.
- `GetObjectAssetData` forwards `Range` and `If-Range` to the prophet and passes its `206` or `416` on. With an `AssetCache` it asks the prophet for the whole body, cuts the range from it while the whole body streams into `Put`, and serves ranges from a stored body; a partial body is never put.
- `client.GetStream` answers a `416` as a response, as it does a `304`.
